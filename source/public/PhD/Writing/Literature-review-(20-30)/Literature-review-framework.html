    Literature review framework

    Literature review framework

    This research examines how fine artists use cinematic genre tropes and visual motifs in moving image work and photography, how authorship is conceptualised when working with techniques including appropriation and pastiche, and how working with AI-generated content complicates these issues further.

    While the deliberate appropriation of existing images, styles, or motifs from other works and cultural sources is a well documented creative method, this approach appears to be an increasing aspect of works which use prompt based and generative ai systems as a tool. Rather than a deliberate critique or contextualisation of the original materials, AI image tools often recreate the visual style of individuals and movements, potentially without the intention, or the understanding of the artist making a new work.  In the context of a technologically engaged visual practice, I’m interested in how artists approach copying, pastiche, homage, remix, appropriation, ripping, quotation and recontextualisation, and where the differences between these practices lie.

    The following is a preliminary outline of topics for a literature review. Some sections are less robust than others, and should be refined over time.

    Appropriation, copying and complicating authorship

    This section works to define what I mean by appropriation in this context, acknowledging that anxiety about the individual artist and machinic reproduction is not new, and grounding appropriation as a method.

    If we compare the intentional process of appropriation as a method to the modelling of a style via a model, are the critical possibilities of appropriation as an artistic method still present if the culture being pastiched is not visible?

    • Appropriation as a way of understanding how ownership, authorship and value is constructed
    • Meme and remix as a way democratise culture
    • Recontextualisation (detournement) as a method to create new meaning
    • Power and credit discrepancies
    • Flatting context and meaning
    • Legal and economic harm to artists
    • Opportunism without criticality
    • Does it matter who is being appropriated (artist, corporation, anonymous author, marginalised community) and the intent of the appropriation.
    • What is the difference between appropriation of the image itself, and the appropriation of a style or vibe?

    Genre, cinematic genre

    Artists have recontextualised images and media from a variety of industries including film, tv, advertising and in order frame my reading in a managable way, I will be considering how artists have used motifs and tropes from cinema specifically. To be able to understand what I mean by appropriating elements of genre, I need to first understand what genre is in cinema, how it is constructed and how it is used.

    Key questions are:

    • What is genre
    • How is genre used
    • Who defines genre and why

    I’m considering whether I need to also include a section on how artists have used cinema in their practices, but this may be expansive.

    Gothic horror

    I have chosen gothic horror as the genre as the primary genre for investigation. ‘Gothic’ as a set of conditions can be applied to music, fashion, literature, games, films and subculture, with can contain further subgenres depending on the area like cyber goth fashion, new and neo gothic art, gothic romance literature. Gothic horror in itself is amorphous and cycles into popular culture often (see 2026’s The Bride!, MĀRAMA, 2025’s Frankenstein, Dracula A loves tale, Sinners (southern gothic), 2024’s Nosferatu).

    Introducing this genre as a focus relates to my practice and personal interested in looking at gothic/horror and genre fiction/ing, and its use as a precursor to science fiction, speculative fiction, and its use as reactive (potentially reactionary) media .

    Here I hope to introduce the definitions of gothic horror historically, contemporarily, in cinema and in art, noting that these areas bleed into one another constantly.

    What’s the difference between gothic horror and horror for goths?
