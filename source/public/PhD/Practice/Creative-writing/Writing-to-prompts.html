    On 25th Aug 26, I asked Claude (Sonnet 5 medium) to give me 6 gothic story premises.

    Here are the premises:

    Here are six gothic story premises:

    1. The Inherited House
    A woman inherits a decaying estate from an aunt she never knew, only to discover the will requires her to live there for a full year before she can sell it. The portraits in the hallway seem to rearrange themselves each night, and the locals refuse to say why the previous owner never left the grounds alive.

    2. The Physician’s Ward
    A doctor at a remote Victorian asylum becomes obsessed with a patient who claims to remember her own death. As he digs into the asylum’s records, he finds his own name in ledgers dating back a hundred years, in handwriting identical to his own.

    3. The Drowned Bride
    On the eve of her wedding, a bride finds an old wedding dress in the attic, still damp, that fits her perfectly. As the wedding approaches, she begins losing time, waking near the lake with no memory, while guests whisper about the groom’s first fiancée, who vanished the same way.

    4. The Cartographer’s Debt
    A mapmaker is hired to survey a fog-bound coastal village that appears in no official records and disappears from any map drawn of it within a week. He accepts a night’s lodging from a gracious family, only to realize the village has been trying to map itself onto him.

    5. The Twin in the Wall
    After her twin sister’s death, a woman moves into their childhood home to settle the estate and starts hearing her sister’s voice through the walls, giving her small, helpful warnings, each one true. She isn’t sure whether she’s being protected or slowly replaced.

    6. The Choir of St. Agnes
    A restoration architect is hired to repair a fire-damaged church organ and finds that playing certain notes summons a choir no one else can hear, singing hymns older than the church itself, always one verse further along than they should be able to know.

    I’m going to write to each prompt for 30 minutes and see what happens.

    I’m going to use a system that I started using with In-grid, but I was first introduced to through Rowena Murray’s writing retreats. Timed writing

    1 (write about anything, but try to write in full sentences) 3 5 9 13

    My hope is that by taking the pressure off of writing, I can start to build some of my practice centered work. It’s a process I’ve used before and it’s worked well.

    5 min: a switch. Numb fingers found what she was looking for but the room remained dim but for pale moonlight pooling through window.     9 min: Instead of braving the mailstrome outside, Anna clattered around the drawers of the small kitchenette until she found a pack of cheap tea lights. Dotting them around as she explored the cottage she got her barrings. The main house, some 15 minutes drive further into the estate, seeming a bit too daunting to visit just now, Anna had instead opted to spend her first night as someone with land in this vastly smaller servants quarter. It felt a little laughable in all honestly. She was currently renting a flat she couldn’t afford, while (nearly) owning a stately home she hadn’t yet set foot in, and sleeping in a shed. Well, the cottage wasn’t that bad. There was one room with a small kitchenette and a space to sit with a hard wearing sofa and a stubby bookshelf. Through one door she found a sink and a chipped porceline bath. In another a toilet, and finally a small bedroom with a single bed on an iron frame. Trying the taps she was pleased to find water running, although it was freezing.

    15 min: A bit too much to ask for the electricity and hot water to be running perhaps, but at least there was indoor plumbing. She could rough it tonight before heading up to the big house tomorrow to meet with Mr Gingridge from the solicitors.
