Good observation! Honestly, a few reasons:

**The AI model itself skews contemporary** — the aesthetic defaults of current video AI (clean kitchens, white shirts, shallow focus, moody underlighting) are trained heavily on modern commercial and streaming content, so the visual language it produces naturally echoes directors who are prominent _now_.

**The styles it can replicate are ones with lots of training data** — Fincher, Villeneuve, Garland etc. have highly distinctive, widely discussed and documented visual signatures that are well represented online, which is exactly what AI models learn from.

**Noir and classic cinema is harder to prompt** — older directors like Kubrick, Cassavetes, Tarkovsky, Bergman, or Ophüls had styles rooted in film grain, anamorphic lenses, and practical lighting that AI video currently struggles to authentically reproduce. When you get B&W it tends to look like _contemporary_ B&W (clean, digital) rather than genuine 1950s or 1960s celluloid.

**I was also pattern-matching to what felt plausible** — since this is AI-generated footage, it made sense to name directors whose work the model might plausibly have "absorbed."

If you wanted to push toward older directors — say Kubrick's symmetry, or Fellini's chaos, or Bresson's austerity — it would be interesting to see how hard you'd have to work against the model's defaults to get there. Have you tried prompting for specific older styles?
