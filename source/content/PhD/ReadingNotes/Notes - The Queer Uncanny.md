**Summary**

The chapter outlines the content of the rest of the book. It introduces how the uncanny can reflect society and its fears and desires, and in particular those of marginalised groups and queer individuals, and in turn how queer phenomenology is used as a way to theoretically approach works which engage with the gothic. It notes the limitations of queer theory, and its ambiguity as a term. She then also touches on how these themes are approached in works centering lesbian and homosexual relationships, particuarly relating to sex, and how it might range in explicitness and obscurity, excess, objectification
