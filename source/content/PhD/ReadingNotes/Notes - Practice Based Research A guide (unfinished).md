**Methods centred on making**

- **Reflective practice / reflection-in and on-action** (Schön) — structured reflection at defined points in the making process, not just diary-keeping. The rigour comes from the structure: fixed intervals, consistent prompts, dated records.
- https://libguides.hull.ac.uk/reflectivewriting/schon
- **Studio/practice journals and process logs** — the primary data source for most projects. Version-controlled repos, commit histories, screen recordings and build notes all count and are more credible than retrospective narrative.
	- Process logs from the repo, can I build that into my record keeping more? 
- **Iterative cycles / action research** — plan, make, observe, reflect, revise. Maps cleanly onto software iteration, which is why it's the default for digital work.
- **Research through design / prototyping** — building successive artefacts where each prototype tests a proposition. Distinguish exploratory prototypes from ones designed to answer a specific question; examiners look for that.
- **Material thinking / thinking-through-making** (Bolt, Carter) — the argument that handling the medium generates knowledge unavailable by other means. In digital work this means the specific affordances and resistances of your toolchain.
- **Autoethnography** — first-person account situated in a cultural context. Useful, but needs a clear analytic frame or it reads as memoir.
- **Speculative and critical design / design fiction** — artefacts as arguments about possible futures rather than solutions.
- **A/r/tography** — artist/researcher/teacher identities held together; strongest if teaching is part of your practice.

**Methods for gathering data around the work**

- **Audience and participant studies** — observation, think-aloud protocols, interaction logging, video analysis of engagement, exit interviews. Standard for interactive installation work, and the thing most often done too thinly.
- **Semi-structured interviews** — with other practitioners, curators, technicians, participants.
- **Elicitation methods** — showing participants the artefact, footage or their own interaction data to prompt reflection.
- **Case study** — of other artists' works, of your own project, or comparative.
- **Practitioner-expert critique / peer review sessions** — documented crits treated as data rather than as informal feedback.
- **Exhibition as method** — the show as a designed research event with defined conditions, not just dissemination.

**Analytic methods**

- **Thematic analysis** and **grounded theory coding** — for interview and reflection data.
- **Interaction analysis** of video and logs — micro-level examination of what people actually did.
- **Contextual review** — the art-and-design equivalent of a literature review, covering works, exhibitions and artefacts alongside texts.
- **Practice mapping / visual methods** — diagrams, matrices and taxonomies as analytic instruments, not illustrations. Gray & Malins is strong on this.
- **Media archaeology / software studies readings** — analysing the technical substrate as culturally loaded.

**Framing and documentation methods**

- **Documentation as method** (Mäkelä, Nimkulrat) — deciding what to capture, when and in what form, as a deliberate methodological choice. For time-based and software work this is load-bearing.
- **Triangulation across modes** — Nelson's point that practice, documentation and writing each evidence different things, and the claim rests on their convergence.
- **Portfolio or exposition structure** — the Research Catalogue's exposition format as a way of presenting multi-media evidence.
